Design rationale

How this login & registration flow applies Jakob Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics and Shneiderman's Eight Golden Rules of Interface Design. Several principles reinforce each other by design — that overlap is intentional, not duplication.

Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics
1

Visibility of system status

Buttons switch to a spinner + "Signing in…" / "Creating your account…" on submit, so you know something is happening.

2

Match between system & the real world

Plain field labels ("Full name", "Course"), familiar icons (envelope, lock, eye), and real course names instead of codes or jargon.

3

User control & freedom

Switching between Login and Register never discards what you've typed in either form. The Forgot Password and Help modals close with the ✕, the backdrop, or Esc — nothing traps you.

4

Consistency & standards

One Bootstrap button style for primary actions, the same show/hide-password control on every password field, and standard patterns (checkbox, toggle chips) throughout.

5

Error prevention

Validation runs live as you type, not only on submit. Confirm-password catches typos, course selection is a tap instead of free text, and the submit button is disabled mid-request to block double-submits.

6

Recognition rather than recall

"Remember me" pre-fills your username next time. Password requirements sit next to the field instead of hidden in a tooltip.

7

Flexibility & efficiency of use

You can jump straight to a view with a direct link (#login, #register, #notes) or bookmark it. Enter submits either form from any field.

8

Aesthetic & minimalist design

Each screen shows only what's needed for that step; secondary content like password rules sits close to where it's needed instead of crowding the form.

9

Recognise, diagnose & recover from errors

Every validation message says what's wrong and how to fix it in plain language, sits right under its field, appears the moment you type something invalid, and clears itself once it's valid.

10

Help & documentation

A persistent "?" button opens a help panel (FAQ accordion) with answers to common questions, without leaving the page.

Shneiderman's eight golden rules
1

Strive for consistency

Shared type scale, spacing, color and components (all from Bootstrap 5) across the login and registration screens.

2

Seek universal usability

Responsive grid down to phone widths, semantic HTML with real <label>s, visible keyboard focus states, and large tap targets.

3

Offer informative feedback

Light actions (picking a course) get a subtle visual change; heavy actions (submitting a form) get a toast or alert confirming exactly what happened.

4

Design dialogs to yield closure

Registration ends with an explicit "Account created" banner and a "Continue to login" button, and login ends on a welcome panel confirming what you're enrolled in.

5

Prevent errors

Inputs are validated the moment you type, not only on submit, and the submit button only activates once the form can realistically succeed.

6

Permit easy reversal of actions

You can re-open "forgot password", close it without sending anything, untoggle a course chip, or sign out with one click.

7

Keep users in control

Nothing navigates or submits on its own — every transition is a direct response to a click or key press, never a timer forcing the pace.

8

Reduce short-term memory load

The password checklist turns green as you satisfy each rule; "remember me" removes the need to retype a username; selected courses stay visible as chips.

Scope note: this is a front-end prototype built for Iqra University's course platform. Accounts are validated and stored in the browser (localStorage) purely to demonstrate the flows above — a real deployment would authenticate and hash passwords on a server, and would never evaluate credentials on the client.
